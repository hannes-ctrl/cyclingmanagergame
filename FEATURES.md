# Features

Everything the game does, in detail. The [README](README.md) has the short version, the [ROADMAP](ROADMAP.md) what comes next and the [CHANGELOG](CHANGELOG.md) what changed. Each point names in brackets the version that brought it, and the versions that changed it much.

## Team

Take over any WorldTeam or ProTeam, or found your own: choose name, a unique abbreviation, colours and country, and pick riders from the whole database, each with his profile. The strength of the best eight decides the licence, the licence the squad size (WorldTeam 27–30, ProTeam 20–30, Continental 16–30); staff, facilities and budget follow the strength of the squad. *(0.1.0; flags 0.3.1, squad sizes 0.4.0, licence rules and profiles 0.6.0)*
- Rider form from a fitness/fatigue model, training load and focus per rider, crashes and illness. Hard training builds more fitness and development, but makes a rider prone to infections and overtraining, the more so when he is tired; the load of the whole squad is set with one click. *(0.1.0; hard training 0.3.1)*
- Monthly development by age and potential: a rider grows mainly where he is already strong, slows down towards a ceiling set by his potential and declines gently from 30, faster from 33. *(0.1.0; slower 0.2.0)*
- Rider search over the whole database: name, team, country, level, age, strength, potential, any single attribute, rider type and contract; riders nobody has scouted show ranges. Filters and column sets can be saved. *(0.2.0; saved filters 0.4.0)*
- Rider page with photo (a neutral silhouette for riders without one), key figures including birth date, height and weight, attributes, the change of each attribute since the rider joined, a twelve-month outlook, the form curve of the season, results, palmares and career seasons. *(0.2.0; body figures 0.3.1)*
- Riders as people. Every rider has a personality of five values (professionalism, ambition, loyalty, consistency, adaptability), summed up in one term as in football management games, from "model professional" and "loyal" through "balanced" to "mercenary", "slack" and "temperamental"; about a fifth are balanced, a fifth difficult. It comes from what the WorldDB knows: loyalty mostly from a rider's contract loyalty and a little from having renewed with his team, professionalism and consistency from his years as a professional, ambition from his wins and, for young riders, from great potential; the best riders are never unambitious or slack. A rider has the same personality in every career, shown for riders your scouts know well. It changes how fast he develops, how strongly he reacts, what he asks to renew, how evenly he performs and how fast he settles in a new team. *(0.3.0; adaptability 0.4.0, from the WorldDB 0.5.0)*
- Morale for the riders of every team: wins and podiums lift it, too few race days lower it, and it shifts form and wage demands. For your riders your decisions move it as well: promises kept or broken, and whether a rider rides in the part his standing lets him expect. A rider who is injured or ill does not blame you for the race he misses. *(0.3.0)*
- Standing in the team (star, leader, key domestique, domestique, prospect): follows from strength and age and can be changed for your riders. A star used as a helper in a race that matters is displeased; a helper allowed to ride for himself is pleased. *(0.3.0)*

The sports directors set them from the season plan, for every team; on the rider page the target changes them, in the rider matrix a right click does, and the plan follows. Goals you set stay yours. Goals of sponsor and board raise the importance of the races they concern. *(0.3.0)*
- Talks with riders as a decision in the inbox: a rider who asks to leave (promise him a leading role, let him go, or refuse), a rider who wants more races. *(0.4.0)*
- Mentoring of young riders by experienced ones, in every team: the mentor's professionalism rubs off. *(0.4.0)*
- Shortlist: a star on the rider page puts a rider on it. A message arrives when the signing period opens and he is available, and when he renews or signs elsewhere; the rider search can show the shortlist alone. *(0.3.0)*

The 24 development teams of the database are linked to their real professional teams; the link never changes, and a team without one (such as a newly founded team) can found its own. Camps for January and February are booked from November. *(0.1.0; linked 0.2.0, signings 0.3.1, winter camps 0.4.0)*

Each level costs upkeep, a new one money and some months of building, both in proportion to the team's budget; two can be built at the same time. When the contracts run out, three offers arrive on 1 September: better material against more money. ## Racing

World and European championships move to another country every season, and races change their course: from the second season, every race for which the WorldDB holds routes of other years (2021 to 2025, about 400 races, the Grand Tours among them) cycles through them in an order fixed per career, never the same course twice in a row. Races can be sorted by importance and by date. *(0.1.0; courses 0.3.1, sorting 0.4.0)*
- Simulate to a date: a click on the date opens a small calendar; optionally the sports director handles line-ups and radio until then. *(0.1.0)*
- Vacation mode in the same calendar: the staff take over training, scouting, the season plan, line-ups and the radio, also over the turn of the year; the assistant answers the riders' talks (more races for one who asks for them, no for one who wants to leave) or you come back for each; a job offer always brings you back. The board never signs or renews riders for you. A save made during the vacation loads with your own settings. *(0.5.0)*
- Season plan by the sports director: before the first day he assigns the riders to every race of the season. The races are planned in the order of their importance (the Tour first, then the other Grand Tours, the monuments, the WorldTour and so on, a race in the team's own country counting more), with the riders who suit the stages best; a stage race gets climbers by its mountains and at most one sprinter, two for a sprint team. Young riders get fewer race days and no or only one Grand Tour, a second Grand Tour is the exception, and of a team's five best Grand Tour riders at most two start together. When a rider is injured or ill, the director takes him out of the races he misses and names a replacement. Every entry can be changed by hand, "Replan the season" starts over, and the whole task can be switched off under "Delegation". The sports directors of all other teams plan their seasons in the same way. *(0.3.0; balanced teams and even race days 0.4.0)*
- The season planner as a timeline in the manner of PCM: races over their real dates, overlaps marked, camps and lay-offs in each rider's row; warnings for more than 85 race days, goal races he is not planned for and too many riders; programme templates (seven built in, own ones from a rider's programme). *(0.4.0)*
- The next season in the winter: from the end of the racing the planner switches to next year, with next year's courses and championship venues and the squad as the signed contracts make it. Riders pencilled in and goals set there become the programme at the turn of the year, for the races the team is invited to; the sports director plans the rest if he is in charge. *(0.5.0)*

"Fix everything" overrides the sports director completely. ## How the simulation works

A stage is cut into 200 m segments; where the profile smooths away the steepest ramp of a categorised climb, the ramp is put back. Riders have a threshold power derived from their attributes for the terrain of each segment, an endurance reserve that drains over the day and a short-term reserve for efforts above threshold. Groups ride on a shared clock; drafting, rolling resistance, gradient, air density and wind decide who can hold a wheel.

- **Body and altitude.** Every rider rides with his own weight and height from the WorldDB. The attributes keep their meaning (watts per kilogram uphill, watts per drag area on the flat), but descents and rolling roads feel the real build. Above 500 m the air gets thinner: less drag, and less power for the riders.
- **Weather.** Temperature, rain and wind follow month and country. The road turns as it goes, so the wind comes from ahead, behind or the side; heat and cold cost energy, rain makes descents slower and crashes more likely.
- **The fight for the break.** The field rides hard until a move sticks: on sprint stages after a few kilometres, on hard days often only after an hour, and on a climb the strongest get away.
- **Sitting up.** On a hard day, once the race is on, a rider with nothing left to ride for lets the group go on the climbs and rolls in with others like him; a helper keeps going as long as his leader is with him and he still has a job. A gruppetto forms in the mountains and on hilly days.
- **The chase.** Only the riders who take turns on the front share its wind, so a field that lets a group go does not catch it by drafting alone. Helpers spend themselves on the front; once empty they drop back and their team stops chasing. A team whose leader is no longer in the field does not chase either.
- **Position.** Before cobbled sectors, the foot of a climb, open roads in the crosswind and in the last kilometres the teams fight for the front: helpers bring their leader up, strong riders hold their place more easily, and staying in front costs energy. Position decides who gets through a narrow sector, who finds shelter in an echelon and how well a sprinter is placed.
- **Sectors.** A race with enough cobbles or gravel (a classic, Strade Bianche, a stage over many sectors) is a *sector race*. On a sector the group is a line in the order of the fight for position. The further back a rider is, the harder he has to ride; when one lets the wheel go, everyone behind him is gapped unless he has the punch to come round. Rough roads wear down riders who are not made for them, so every sector counts towards the end, and the finale starts at the first very hard sector of the second half, or where half of the sectors are done.
- **Sports directors.** During the race every team is run by the same logic, the player's team included. A team joins the chase when the gap gets out of hand and it has a favourite to ride for. A rider in the break does not pull when his leader is a favourite behind. A team that loses its leader lets its best remaining rider ride for himself. At the start of the finale each team decides whether to sacrifice its helpers, to send its leader on the attack because he would lose a sprint, or to keep things together because he would win it. Dropped favourites are brought back by their helpers. For the player's team each decision can be overruled over the radio, and in a stage watched live each rider can be given an order; whatever the player leaves alone is decided as for every other team. Each team races in its style; with a strong crosswind in the forecast the teams with the strongest rouleurs set out to split the field.
- **Attacks.** Favourites attack on climbs and cobbles, late on the flat and, if they descend well, downhill; a clearly stronger rider may go from far out. In the break the riders attack each other as the finish nears. Every rider descends at his own speed.
- **Crashes and punctures.** They happen during the stage, more often in a nervous bunch, on descents, on cobbles and in the rain. Helpers wait for a leader and pace him back behind the team cars; a crash can cost form for days or end the race. Inside the last three kilometres a rider keeps the time of his group. Tyres and mechanics count: wide tyres puncture less on cobbles, narrow ones more; a good mechanic halves part of the time lost. When a leader crashes or punctures, his team decides how many helpers wait.
- **Sprints** with lead-out trains, position and the occasional crash; **uphill finishes** ridden with whatever is left.
- **Time trials** with start order and a wind that changes over the day; fatigue counts. A team time trial is ridden in a line; whoever cannot hold the wheel is let go, and the time is taken on the fourth or fifth rider. The bike of the day counts: the time trial bike is faster on the flat, the lighter road bike can be faster on a mountain time trial.
- **Classifications.** Times count in whole seconds, minus the bonus seconds at the finish and at the sprints; the team classification adds the three best of each team, and a team with fewer than three riders at the line drops out of it for good.
- **Stage races.** Fatigue grows with the effort of the day and shrinks by a share that depends on the recovery attribute, more on rest days. Riders outside the time limit leave the race unless more than a fifth of the field is; the gruppetto rides for its weakest riders to stay inside it, and every group far behind watches the limit too.

Each team rides to a plan: a role per rider, an approach (wait, control, make the race hard) and a breakaway policy. AI teams derive their plan from the route, their riders and the general classification. The quality of the sports director in the car changes how well the plan is executed.

- **In the order of the race clock.** The groups are ridden one step at a time, always the one that is furthest behind in time, so the simulation can stop at any moment of the race, take an order or an answer and go on. A group that splits off mid-step is ridden at once up to the others. A stage ridden in one go and one stopped a hundred times come out the same.
- **In the background.** In the game the stages of a day are simulated on other isolates, all races of the day at the same time, while the window stays free; the header shows what is being computed. A stage is handed over as a job (`StageJob`) holding everything that decides it, so the result is the same as on the main thread. Watching a stage, skipping it and simulating to a date all go through `StageRaceRun`; tests check both.

Everything is deterministic for a given seed. 48 tuning parameters live in `data/calibration/tuning.json`. The physical ones (threshold power, the gain per attribute point, drafting, the spread of the daily form) are held within values from the literature, so that the calibration tunes how teams race rather than physics.

## How the calibration works

The calibration package compares the simulation with real WorldTour results of the seasons 2023 to 2026 and searches for better tuning values (`calibrate.ps1`, typically overnight). The results are not part of the repository: `dart run bin/fetch_results.dart <year>` in `packages/calibration` loads a season into `data/results/<year>/`, and the script does that itself before every run. Races of earlier seasons are simulated on the route of their year (`worlddb variants`) with the riders of the real result who are in the database, at their strength of 2026.

The real race is measured on the riders the simulation has, so that both count the same field. Only the dynamics of a race are scored, by five figures: the size of the winner's group, the gap of tenth place, the gap of the middle of the field, the average speed, and whether the early breakaway won. Further terms check how often one of the three riders best suited to the route wins, per race type (only in 2026, whose riders the ratings describe), how many sprinters finish outside the time limit on hard stages of stage races, and how much time the ten best of the general classification lose on a stage. Which rider wins is deliberately not part of the score: the best riders do not start everywhere, and crashes, illness and form of the day decide a single real result. That is also why older seasons are usable although their fields were different.

The error has three parts: an error per race in those figures and the time loss of the classification leaders (capped, except for the breakaway), per race type the difference in how often a breakaway wins and how often the winner's group falls into each size class (solo, duo, small group, bunch), which keeps the search from giving up on rare outcomes, and per race type the difference in how often a top favourite wins. The race types are flat, hilly, mountain, sector races and time trials; the frequency terms weight them by the square root of their number of races, so that the few sector races are not sacrificed. The real weather of a race day is not known; each run draws one for the country and the month.

The earlier a season, the less of its field the database covers, because it knows the riders of 2026: of the real finishers of 2021 only 58 % are found, of 2023 75 %, of 2025 92 % (`dart run bin/seasons.dart`). Those fields are smaller in the simulation than they were, and a fifth to a third of their riders were much younger then or are past their best now. The calibration therefore uses the seasons from 2023 on (`firstSeason` in `dataset.dart`), 534 races, and the report lists the error per season. With `--from 2021` the earlier seasons come back: an anonymous rider takes the place of each finisher the database does not know, typical of his team in that race (the median of his known teammates), never judged by where he finished. The search changes about three tuning values per attempt. The real figures are measured on all finishers, so a winner who has since retired still counts; a big first group is scaled to the part of the field that is simulated. A fifth of the races is held back as a control set, and an improvement only counts if it holds on a second set of random numbers. Races that cannot be matched, such as parade stages, are listed in `data/calibration/exclude.txt`. The report also names tuning values that sit at the edge of their range.
